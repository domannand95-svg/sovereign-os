# Authority-Epistemics Separation

## Governing principle

> Intelligence may propose. Epistemics may evaluate. Only authority may permit.

The non-escalation invariant is:

> No property of a proposal or evaluation implicitly grants permission, and every transition into authority must occur through an explicit, inspectable, auditable boundary.

This baseline consolidates the separation already required by ADR-0002, the governed research-artifact contract, and the registry admission contracts. It does not activate a new runtime capability or claim that every future authority role is implemented.

## Terms

**Intelligence** produces candidate content: objectives, claims, plans, artifacts, tool requests, policy amendments, grants, or promotion requests. A candidate may be useful, correct, well sourced, highly rated, or generated by a trusted provider without acquiring authority.

**Epistemics** produces and relates evidence about a candidate: sources, methods, uncertainty, failed attempts, reviewer findings, disputes, reproducibility results, and evidence dispositions. A favorable finding or disposition is evidence for a later governed decision; it is not permission to execute, publish, promote, grant, or mutate canonical state.

**Authority** is a versioned, scoped permission exercised by an identified decision-maker under an identified policy. In the implemented registry path, authoritative resolution and deterministic policy evaluation meet at explicit admission gates. In the implemented engine path, a `DirectivePolicy` decision precedes preflight, durable append, and publication. These are distinct policy surfaces and neither should be inferred from epistemic status.

**External consequence** means an effect beyond constructing or evaluating a candidate, including canonical-state mutation, ledger append, registry admission, tool execution, filesystem or network mutation, resource consumption, disclosure, promotion, grant issuance, delegation, revocation, or constitutional change.

## Required separation

The following implications are invalid unless an explicit authority boundary separately establishes permission:

- proposal quality, confidence, popularity, provider identity, or elapsed service time implies authority;
- source reputation, citation count, reviewer agreement, successful reproduction, or evidence disposition implies authority;
- an admitted evidence record implies permission to execute the method or requested effect;
- registry identity, lineage, presence, or schema validity implies operational permission;
- a capability-shaped payload, tool registration, plugin registration, or available executor implies permission to invoke it;
- a successful prior decision implies a transferable ambient credential; or
- replayability, auditability, or deterministic reconstruction makes an originally unauthorized effect authorized.

## Consequential transition record

Every transition into authority must make the following inspectable and reconstructable, either in one canonical record or through immutable references:

- the proposal or requested transition and its subject;
- the initiating actor and asserted authority;
- the evidence and evaluation considered, including unresolved adverse findings required by policy;
- the exact policy, admission context, and authority-state references used;
- the deciding authority and required approvals;
- the permitted operation, target, scope, budget, activation point, and expiry;
- the resulting effect or denial; and
- supersession, suspension, revocation, appeal, and replay links where applicable.

Missing, ambiguous, stale, unresolved, or unverifiable authority information fails closed. Reconstruction demonstrates what decision occurred and under which inputs; it does not replace independent authorization.

## Composition and registration

Composition answers what components can be assembled. Governed capability answers what an identified subject may exercise with consequence. The two questions remain separate.

Registering or mounting a tool, plugin, model adapter, policy evaluator, storage provider, scheduler, or executor must not grant permission merely by making a service reachable. Any composition mechanism able to add, replace, or remove a consequential path is itself a governed operation. Its authority, scope, policy, actor, configuration change, activation point, and reversal must be recorded. Unmounting a component can reverse runtime wiring, but cannot erase the historical authority decision or the evidence of effects already exercised.

## Relationship to current implementation

The authoritative workspace already implements deterministic directive admission, registry schema and lineage validation, authoritative issuer and governing-policy resolution, Gate 6 authorization, durable append, replay, restoration, and projection-tail checks. These mechanisms enforce parts of this baseline.

The workspace does not yet implement the complete research-evaluation, grant, tool-execution, promotion, revocation, and institutional-authority lifecycle described by ADR-0002 and the delivery backlog. Documentation for those future roles remains a constraint on later schemas and policy work, not evidence that the runtime enforces them today.

## Verification rule

A future conformance claim requires negative tests showing that favorable proposal or evaluation properties cannot bypass the relevant authority boundary, and that the independently reconstructed decision record binds the same subject, policy, scope, inputs, result, and consequence as the live transition.
